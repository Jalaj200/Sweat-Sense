About The Project

Bringing Sweat-Based Biosensing Into Focus

CRPify is dedicated to investigating how human sweat can serve as a rich biofluid for biosensing, and how C-reactive protein (CRP) detection could support future research into non-invasive or minimally invasive biomarker monitoring.

Sweat-Based Sample Analysis

Examining the physiological composition of eccrine sweat, passive and stimulated excretion dynamics, and target biomarker partitioning from blood capillaries.

Biosensor Development

Designing conceptual microfluidic sample routing architectures combined with bioreceptor functionalized surfaces for selective analyte recognition.

Biomarker Detection Research

Studying molecular interactions, binding kinetics, cross-reactivity challenges, and signal transduction methods specifically tailored for low-volume biofluids.

Wearable Monitoring Concepts

Envisioning future wearable form factors that could one day enable continuous, point-of-care physiological research without invasive venous punctures.

Research Scope vs. Clinical Validation

CRPify explicitly distinguishes between preliminary research objectives and clinically validated diagnostic devices. Our focus is fundamental biosensing modeling, scientific inquiry, and sensor interface engineering.

Biological Significance

Why Detect C-Reactive Protein?

C-reactive protein (CRP) is one of the most widely investigated biomarkers in biomedical science, serving as a key systemic indicator of acute and chronic inflammation.

Biomarker Profile Molecular Structure: Pentameric Protein

What is CRP?

C-reactive protein (CRP) is a homopentameric acute-phase reactant protein synthesized primarily by hepatocytes in the liver. Its production is rapidly triggered in response to pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 (IL-6).

  • Acute-Phase Indicator: Circulating levels in blood plasma can surge dramatically within hours of inflammatory stimuli or tissue damage.
  • Nonspecific Biomarker: An elevated CRP measurement signals the presence of systemic inflammation but does not diagnose any specific medical condition on its own.
  • Clinical Context: Widely assessed in cardiovascular risk stratification, infection tracking, and autoimmune research.
Sampling Medium Eccrine Biofluid Dynamics

Why Sweat?

Human sweat provides a compelling non-invasive biofluid containing electrolytes, metabolites, hormones, and proteins that can potentially be harvested without needles or invasive clinical equipment.

  • Continuous Non-Invasive Access: Sweat can be sampled continuously over extended periods using wearable skin-conformal sensor patches.
  • Scientific Challenges: CRP exists in sweat at concentrations significantly lower than in blood plasma, requiring ultra-sensitive detection limits.
  • Threshold Interpretation: Established clinical blood CRP reference ranges (e.g. <3 mg/L) cannot be directly applied to sweat without rigorous analytical and physiological correlation.
Conceptual Architecture

How the Detection Process Works

A four-phase conceptual workflow modeling sample harvesting, microfluidic transport, bioreceptor interaction, and quantitative signal transduction.

01
Phase 1

Sweat Collection

A skin-contact sensor interface receives natural or iontophoretically stimulated eccrine sweat via hydrophilic micro-wells, avoiding epidermal contamination.

02
Phase 2

Sample Interaction

Capillary action directs the sweat volume through microfluidic channels designed to regulate flow velocity and minimize sample evaporation during transit.

03
Phase 3

CRP Recognition

Target C-reactive protein molecules selectively interact with an immobilized bio-recognition layer (such as anti-CRP antibodies or DNA/RNA aptamers).

04
Phase 4

Signal Analysis

Transducer circuitry translates molecular binding events into impedance or amperometric changes, analyzing signal variance against calibrated baseline models.

Research Note: This four-step model represents a proposed scientific workflow. Quantitative performance metrics (detection limits, response times, sensitivity) must derive from empirical wet-lab validation.

Scientific Limitations & Responsible Use

Transparent scientific inquiry is essential when exploring novel biosensing modalities. Visitors and researchers must note the following critical boundaries:

Sweat Volume & Composition Variance

Sweat generation rates, salinity, and pH vary substantially across individuals, ambient temperatures, and hydration states, which directly impacts biofluid sampling consistency.

Sweat vs. Blood CRP Dissimilarity

Physiological levels of C-reactive protein in sweat are typically orders of magnitude lower than in blood plasma, and partition dynamics remain an active subject of ongoing biomedical investigation.

Calibration & Interference Rigor

Reliable quantification requires multi-point calibration, nonspecific binding mitigation, and strict analytical validation under controlled laboratory testing.

Non-Diagnostic Classification

Research findings from CRPify must never be interpreted as a clinical diagnosis. Medical decisions must never be based on an unvalidated prototype or experimental calculation.
